For parents

Could my child benefit from OT?

A paediatric occupational therapist helps children with the everyday “jobs” of childhood: playing, learning, getting dressed, eating and making friends. For your child, therapy looks a lot like play, and it’s built around what matters to your family.

first, a breath Every child develops at their own pace. One sign on its own usually isn’t a reason to worry — but if you notice a pattern, or something keeps getting in the way of everyday life at home or school, it’s worth checking.

Signs to look for, by age

Babies & toddlers

0–3 years

You might notice that your little one…

  • HandsDoesn’t often reach for, hold or pass toys from one hand to the other.
  • MovementRolls, sits, crawls or walks later than expected, or their body seems very floppy or stiff.
  • SensesGets very upset by everyday sounds, bath time, nail cutting or being held — or seems to need to move all the time.
  • EatingFinds it hard to move on to lumpy food, to chew, or to drink from a cup, and gags often.
  • SettlingIs very hard to settle or comfort, most days.
  • PlayShows little interest in toys, faces or back-and-forth games like peekaboo.

Preschool

3–6 years

You or the crèche teacher might notice that your child…

  • HandsFinds it hard to hold a crayon, cut with scissors, or do up big buttons and zips.
  • MovementOften trips or bumps into things, or avoids climbing, jumping and ball games.
  • SensesIs upset by clothing labels, haircuts, messy hands or noisy places — or is always on the move, crashing and spinning.
  • Self-careNeeds much more help than other children their age with dressing, using a spoon and fork, or the toilet.
  • Feelings & focusHas big, frequent meltdowns, or finds it very hard to sit for a short story or activity.
  • Play & friendsMostly plays alone, or finds taking turns and joining in hard.
  • Getting ready for schoolIsn’t drawing simple shapes yet, or switches hands a lot, by Grade R.

School age

6–12 years

You or the teacher might notice that your child…

  • HandwritingWrites slowly or untidily, gets tired quickly, or finds it hard to form and space letters.
  • School tasksStruggles every day with copying from the board, cutting, colouring in, or keeping their desk and bag organised.
  • MovementSeems clumsy in sport, or finds riding a bike, catching a ball or skipping hard.
  • SensesIs very bothered by noise, touch or certain foods and clothes — or needs to fidget and move to concentrate.
  • Self-careStill needs a lot of help with buttons, shoelaces, using a knife, or getting ready in the morning.
  • Feelings & focusFinds it hard to start a task, follow instructions, or calm down after being upset.
  • Play & friendsFinds friendships, group games or losing a game harder than other children their age.

These lists are a guide, not a diagnosis. If you’re worried about your child’s health or development, please also speak to your doctor or clinic sister.

What happens next

If you’d like Robin to have a look

You don’t need a referral. If you’re unsure whether OT is the right place to start, a short WhatsApp is a good first step — Robin will tell you honestly.

  1. A first assessmentAn unhurried first session at home or at school. You talk about your child; Robin watches them play and try everyday tasks, and explains what she sees.
  2. Goals with you and the teacherTogether you agree on a few goals that matter in daily life, in plain language. With your permission, Robin talks to your child’s teacher too, so home and school pull in the same direction.
  3. A home programmeSessions are play-based. Between visits you get simple activities that fit into family life, and the plan is reviewed and adjusted as you go.
