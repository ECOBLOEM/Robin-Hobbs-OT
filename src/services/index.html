01

Childrenpaediatric intervention

For children, therapy looks a lot like play — because play is how children learn. Robin works with you and your child’s teachers so new skills carry over into home and the classroom.

Robin begins training in Sensory Integration in 2027.

Fine motor skills & handwriting

You might notice

  • An awkward or very tight pencil grip
  • Tired hands, or slow and messy writing
  • Trouble with scissors, buttons, zips or laces

How OT helps

Strengthening little hands through play, working on pencil grip and letter formation, and practical tips for the classroom.

Sensory processing

You might notice

  • Big reactions to noise, clothing labels, haircuts or messy hands
  • Always on the move — crashing, spinning, chewing
  • Very fussy eating linked to textures

How OT helps

Understanding how your child takes in the world, then building activities and routines that help them feel calm, safe and ready to learn.

School readiness

You might notice

  • Difficulty sitting at a table or following instructions
  • Not yet holding a crayon or cutting with scissors
  • Worries about starting Grade R or Grade 1

How OT helps

A clear picture of the skills your child already has, targeted activities for the ones still coming, and guidance for parents and teachers.

Developmental delay

You might notice

  • Milestones like sitting, crawling or walking arriving later
  • Clumsiness, or avoiding climbing and ball games
  • Difficulty with feeding, dressing or play

How OT helps

An assessment of movement, play and self-care, then play-based therapy — working with your doctor, physiotherapist or speech therapist where needed.

Attention & emotional regulation

You might notice

  • Finding it hard to sit still or focus
  • Big meltdowns, especially around changes in routine
  • Difficulty settling to sleep or calming down

How OT helps

Practical strategies to calm and focus — movement breaks, visual routines and coaching for parents — that fit into a real family day.

Play, social & self-care skills

You might notice

  • Difficulty taking turns or joining in with other children
  • Struggling to dress, eat with utensils or brush teeth
  • Frustration with everyday routines

How OT helps

Breaking each skill into small steps, practising them through play, and giving you simple ways to keep going at home.

02

Adultsphysical & neurological rehab

For adults and older people, therapy is practical: getting back to the tasks that make up your day — at home, at work, in your community — safely and as independently as possible.

Stroke rehabilitation

You might notice

  • Weakness or clumsiness in one arm or hand
  • Difficulty washing, dressing or cooking
  • Changes in memory, attention or planning

How OT helps

Retraining arm and hand use, relearning daily tasks step by step, adapting the home, and showing family members how best to help.

Neurological conditions

For example

  • Parkinson’s disease or multiple sclerosis
  • Head and brain injury
  • Nerve injuries affecting the arm or hand

How OT helps

Keeping you doing what matters for as long as possible — with adapted techniques, energy-saving strategies and the right equipment.

Hand & arm injuries, fractures and after surgery

You might notice

  • Stiffness, swelling or weakness after a fracture or operation
  • Pain when gripping, lifting or typing
  • Difficulty with fine tasks like buttons or writing

How OT helps

Graded exercises for movement and strength, swelling and scar care, and a step-by-step return to everyday tasks — in line with your doctor’s or surgeon’s guidance.

Daily living & home safety

You might notice

  • Difficulty getting in and out of the bath, bed or a chair
  • Worry about falls
  • Tasks taking much longer than they used to

How OT helps

A home visit to spot risks, practical changes to the space, advice on assistive devices like grab rails or bath boards, and easier ways to do things.

Return to work

Useful when

  • You’re recovering from an injury or illness
  • Your job has physical or mental demands you’re unsure about

How OT helps

Looking closely at what your job asks of you, building a graded plan back, and suggesting practical adjustments where they’d help.

Support for carers & family

Useful when

  • You’re caring for someone at home
  • You’re unsure how to help without doing everything for them

How OT helps

Hands-on guidance with safe transfers and daily routines, and ideas to support independence — while looking after yourself too.
